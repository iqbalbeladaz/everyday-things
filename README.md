## The entry

> A queue is a caterpillar made of people, shuffling forward one tiny step at a time whenever its head moves.
>
> It maps how the whole line moves from the front, one step at a time, but leaves out that, unlike a real caterpillar, nobody is attached and anyone can leave, join, or pick another line.

Two lines, one everyday thing (a queue), explained to a 10-year-old through a
single concrete, surprising analogy — a caterpillar made of people — followed
by one honest sentence about what the analogy maps and what it leaves out.

## Why this analogy

- **Concrete and surprising:** a caterpillar is something a 10-year-old can
  see, hold, and watch crawl; a queue made of people as one long caterpillar
  is a picture nobody has met before.
- **One metaphor, whole picture:** line 1 is a single sentence with no list of
  comparisons and no second metaphor crowding it.
- **Honest second line:** it names exactly what the analogy covers (the whole
  line moves from the front, one step at a time) and what it leaves out
  (unlike a real caterpillar, nobody is attached — anyone can leave, join, or
  pick another line), instead of praising the analogy.
- **No wrong idea left standing:** the mapping's biggest gap — attachment —
  is called out in the open, so the vivid picture cannot mislead.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`. It is fully
deterministic: the same bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no
network. The entry is 294 characters (1–400 required by the gate).

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
